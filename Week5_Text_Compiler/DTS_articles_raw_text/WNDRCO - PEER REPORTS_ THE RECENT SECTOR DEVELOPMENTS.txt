WndrCo, founded in 2017 by entrepreneur Jeffrey Katzenberg and former Disney executive David Ellison, is a Los Angeles-based holding company dedicated to acquiring control stakes in underappreciated technology firms and investing in innovative startups to accelerate their growth into market leaders. Its core business activities encompass strategic investments, operational enhancements, and scaling support across consumer and enterprise technology sectors, with a focus on lead institutional funding in post-product-market-fit rounds and seed-stage opportunities for next-generation entrepreneurs. Notable portfolio companies include Aura, offering comprehensive identity and financial protection services; Writer, an enterprise AI writing assistant; and Yassir, a leading ride-hailing and delivery platform in the Middle East and Africa. Among its fastest-growing segments, online security solutions-exemplified by Aura and Point Wild''s portfolio of protective brands-stand out for surging demand amid rising cyber threats, while enterprise AI tools like Writer are rapidly expanding, capitalizing on the global shift toward generative technologies and automation. Through these initiatives, WndrCo drives transformative value, fostering innovation and market dominance in high-potential domains.

+ IDILIO SECURES $5.5M SEED BACKING FROM WNDRCO TO ACCELERATE LATIN AMERICAN MICRODRAMA
WndrCo led a $5.5 million seed round for Idilio, a Latin American microdrama platform, with the announcement coming on 4th June 2026. The investment underscores WndrCo's ongoing interest in tech driven entertainment ventures that blend storytelling with scalable software. 
Idilio was co-founded by Gabriela Tafur and Esteban Ramirez and is positioned at the intersection of traditional telenovelas and vertical video formats, powered by an AI engine designed to reduce production costs and timelines. 
Since its launch five months ago, the platform has achieved 1.5 million downloads in 93 countries and streamed 40 million episodes. Users are reportedly watching about 50 minutes of Idilio content per day, a level of engagement that stands out in the mobile video space. 
Idilio Creators will enable creators to monetize directly on the platform, with 100% of profits going to the creators. The program aims to nurture a Latin American content community that can compete with global players by delivering localized stories. 
In addition to WndrCo, the round drew participation from investors such as a16z Speedrun, Goodwater Capital, Precursor Ventures, and fintech executive David Velez, alongside other backers. The backing positions Idilio to scale its AI driven production model across the region.

+ INDIA QUOTIENT AND GENERAL CATALYST BACK OMLI'S SEED ROUND FOR CHILD-FOCUSED AI SPEECH TECHNOLOGY
Gurugram-based Omli has closed a seed round totaling $1.5 million, led by India Quotient with participation from General Catalyst, the company announced on June 17, 2026. The funding will back Omli's work on AI infrastructure for children's speech and language development.
Founders say most existing speech models are trained on adult voices, creating gaps when interacting with children whose speech patterns differ in tone and pace.
Omli's flagship OmliKids product lets children aged three to ten converse with an AI companion named Doro, positioning the app as a conversational platform rather than a game-based experience. The product has already crossed 500,000 downloads on Google Play within a few months of launch.
To address the shortcomings of existing models, Omli has developed VedAnsh-v1, a speech-to-text and voice activity detection system tailored for children's voices. In tests with kid speech, the VedAnsh-v1 engine delivered lower word error rates than many commercial systems. Co-founder Siddhant Garg says the team aims to reach adult-grade accuracy for children's speech, including support for Indian accents and vernacular languages.
With about 15 employees, Omli is working on the next generation of its speech model and plans to extend into conversational intelligence that adapts in real time to a child's speaking pace and communication style. The startup also intends to develop screening tools to help parents and educators identify articulation challenges early. Shivam Munshi notes that many children today are silent on screens, underscoring the need for technology that encourages verbal interaction. Investors see potential in this underserved market, with India Quotient partner Madhukar describing children as the largest consumer segment for which AI solutions are still lacking.
+ RYLO LANDS $85 MILLION GROWTH INVESTMENT LED BY GENERAL CATALYST, FUELING EXPANSION BEYOND $100 MILLION IN FUNDING
Rylo, formerly known as Nagish, has secured $85 million in growth funding in a round led by General Catalyst's Customer Value Fund, with participation from Canaan, Vertex Ventures, and Contour. The investment lifts total funding beyond $100 million and signals strong appetite for AI-powered accessibility solutions.
Based in Tel Aviv and New York, Rylo builds an AI-enabled communication platform designed to improve accessibility for Deaf and hard-of-hearing individuals. Its tools provide real-time captioning for phone calls, rapid speech-to-text conversion, text-to-speech capabilities, and in-person transcription, enabling smoother interactions across personal, professional, and public settings.
Rylo has broadened its product ecosystem through innovation and acquisitions, most notably acquiring Sign.mt in 2025 and launching Rylo Sign for real-time translation between spoken and sign languages. The platform also integrates speaker recognition, sentiment analysis, and fraud detection to enhance usability and accuracy across channels.
Investors view Rylo as a strong player in the fast-growing accessibility tech sector, addressing a long-standing market need by leveraging AI to deliver scalable, cost-effective communication solutions. With regulations evolving and digital communication increasing in importance, demand for barrier-free tools is rising worldwide.
The fresh capital will support expansion of engineering, product development, and commercial operations, while enabling further AI research and infrastructure development and the deployment of new workplace accessibility solutions. Rylo plans to refine its AI models, broaden its toolset, and pursue a long-term strategy of global expansion and wider adoption among individuals and enterprise users.
+ ROBOTICS STARTUP STANDARD BOTS SECURES UNICORN VALUATION WITH $200 MILLION FUNDING
Standard Bots has joined unicorn ranks after closing a $200 million Series C round co-led by General Catalyst and RoboStrategy, valuing the New York-based robotics company at $1 billion. The funding was announced on 10th June 2026.
The company was founded in 2011 by Evan Beard, and David Golden joined as co-founder in 2020. Golden, noted for his work with Bowery Farming and LeapPay, has helped steer the company through its latest growth phase.
The AI-native robotic arms learn by demonstration, letting operators teach tasks directly and shaving deployment times and costs. The technology supports use across machining, welding and assembly, with claimed costs about 30% lower than legacy providers. Customers include Amazon, Lockheed Martin, NASA, the U.S. Army and a broad base of SMBs.
Standard Bots operates a vertically integrated model, designing actuators and assembling most products in-house, with plans to domestically manufacture nearly all components by 2027 to bolster U.S. manufacturing capabilities. Beard has become an advocate for industrial efficiency, briefing policymakers on the value of tech-enabled productivity.
The $200 million round will fund expansion of the Glen Cove facility to 70,000 square feet and support a target of capturing 10% of new U.S. industrial robot deployments this year, even as the company faces competition from established domestic suppliers and a rapid pace of robot installations in China.

+ ANDREESSEN HOROWITZ CRYPTO ARM LEADS $100 MILLION INVESTMENT IN DIGITAL ASSET'S CANTON
On Jun. 21, a16z Crypto announced a $100 million investment in Digital Asset, the San Francisco based company behind the Canton blockchain, highlighting privacy as a central driver of the deal.
The move reflects a broader pivot in blockchain investing, with privacy technology increasingly seen as essential for institutions that once preferred transparent ledgers. Regulators, asset managers, and retail users are seeking confidentiality to protect transactional data while still enabling trusted networks.
Market context frames the rationale: private privacy coins have drawn attention as demand grows. Monero recently surpassed 100% gains over the past year, while Zcash has posted substantial rallies at times, underscoring the appetite for privacy solutions.
Canton’s CC token has benefited from this trend, with a market capitalization exceeding $6.3 billion, ranking around the 15th largest crypto by CoinMarketCap. CC trades near $0.16, with a circulating supply of about 38.8 billion and no fixed maximum supply.
Ali Yahya, a16z Crypto general partner, stated in a blog post that "privacy will be the most important moat in crypto this year," underscoring the firm’s view that confidential transactions will drive broader adoption. The investment places a16z Crypto at the vanguard of a privacy focused shift in digital finance.
Taken together, the Canton investment signals a growing industry consensus that privacy is not a niche feature but a foundational requirement for future growth and institutional participation.
+ CONVEY LANDS $38M SERIES A TO SCALE NON-CODING AI WORKFORCE FOR ENTERPRISES
SAN FRANCISCO--Convey, the enterprise AI platform that enables non-technical operators to build and manage digital teammates that execute business operations autonomously, today announced $38 million in Series A funding led by Andreessen Horowitz (a16z) with continued participation from Khosla Ventures and Pear VC. A16z partner Joe Schmidt will join Convey's board. This announcement comes on 19th June 2026.
The funding highlights rising demand for Convey's platform, which has logged over one million hours of automated work completed for NBCUniversal, Samsara, TelevisaUnivision, Unity, Faire, and ChargePoint.
The capital will support expansion into enterprise markets and product development as the global AI spend is projected to reach $2.5 trillion in 2026.
Convey's core proposition is the ability for teams to onboard and manage their own AI workforce without coding, enabling digital teammates to learn tasks, gather context, and take ownership of responsibilities previously managed manually.
Operators can deploy the teammates to automate tasks such as processing invoices, managing campaign reporting, and ingesting advertising assets at scale, freeing up time for strategic work.
Convey emphasizes governance and security through partnerships with IT departments and critical systems, seeking to deliver measurable returns from the outset and to help enterprises transition to AI native operations.
+ ANDREESSEN HOROWITZ BETS $25M ON STITCH TO DRIVE REGIONAL FINTECH EXPANSION
Stitch, a Saudi financial services startup, has raised a $25 million funding round from Andreessen Horowitz, announced on 17th June 2026. The investment underscores growing investor interest in Middle East fintech platforms and supports Stitch's expansion ambitions.
"It is a significant investment, and it is a big deal for us, and there’s no shying away from that. It does represent tremendous validation for us, and our business model. It’s not just a Stitch story, it’s a regional one," said Mohamed Oueida, Founder and CEO of Stitch. The funds will be used to accelerate growth across the Gulf Cooperation Council, Egypt, and Southeast Asia, including recent launches in the Philippines.
Oueida described the banking technology landscape as dominated by legacy systems and fragmentation that suppress returns on technology spend. "Capital isn’t the problem here; most financial institutions are huge spenders on technology. However, the ROI they are getting from these investments are not where they should be," he said.
Stitch pitches a unified platform to streamline operations and reduce fragmentation. "Today, building software is as easy as making food. We believe that you can build a unified platform on a single operating system, rather than having 1,000 systems running," he added, arguing that AI gains hinge on removing fragmentation.
LuLu Exchange in the UAE has been cited as a partner that helped propel product launches and open doors to new markets, including the Philippines. "After the successful launch of the multiple currency cards, they came back to us and said they needed to think about their next evolution," Oueida noted.
