General Catalyst completed a landmark transaction on June 30, 2025, as Janus Henderson Group Ltd. finalized its take-private deal involving Trian Fund Management, General Catalyst Group Management, and Qatar Investment Authority. Under the terms of the agreement, Janus Henderson's public shares were converted into cash at $52.00 per share and subsequently delisted from the New York Stock Exchange. The firm will continue operating under its current management structure, with Ali Dibadj remaining at the helm as Chief Executive Officer, maintaining operational bases in London and Denver. Dibadj described the transition as a transformative chapter for the firm, citing the opportunity to deepen its legacy in asset management through partnership with Trian and General Catalyst. Nelson Peltz, CEO of Trian, acknowledged the progress Janus Henderson had made since Trian's initial investment in 2020. General Catalyst's CEO Hemant Taneja stated the firm's commitment to enhancing Janus Henderson's technological capabilities and overall growth strategy. The transaction was supported by a broad investor group that included MassMutual and Sun Hung Kai & Co., with legal and financial advisory services provided by leading industry firms.
On June 17, 2025, Gurugram-based artificial intelligence startup Omli announced it had raised $1.5 million in a seed funding round led by India Quotient, with participation from General Catalyst. Founded in 2025 by Shivam Munshi and Siddhant Garg, Omli develops AI infrastructure tailored to children's speech and language development — a segment largely underserved by existing commercial speech recognition systems, which are predominantly trained on adult voices. The company's flagship product, OmliKids, allows children between the ages of three and ten to engage in voice-based conversations with an AI companion named Doro. Within months of its Google Play Store launch, the application surpassed 500,000 downloads. To address the technical limitations of existing models, Omli developed VedAnsh-v1, a proprietary speech-to-text and voice activity detection system engineered specifically for children's voices, demonstrating measurable improvements in word error rates. Co-founder Siddhant Garg stated the company's goal of achieving adult-grade accuracy in children's speech recognition, including support for Indian accents and vernacular languages. The company, which currently employs approximately 15 people, plans to develop next-generation speech models and conversational intelligence systems capable of adapting in real time to individual children's communication patterns. India Quotient's Madhukar noted that children represent the largest consumer segment for which AI solutions remain largely undeveloped.
General Catalyst also led an $85 million growth funding round for Rylo, formerly known as Nagish, through its Customer Value Fund, with participation from Canaan, Vertex Ventures, and Contour. The round pushed Rylo's total funding beyond $100 million. Headquartered across Tel Aviv and New York, Rylo develops AI-powered communication solutions for Deaf and hard-of-hearing individuals, offering real-time captioning for phone calls, speech-to-text and text-to-speech conversion, and in-person transcription services. Following its acquisition of Sign.mt in 2025, the company launched Rylo Sign, enabling real-time translation between spoken and sign languages. The platform also incorporates speaker recognition, sentiment analysis, and fraud detection capabilities. The newly raised capital is intended to support expansion of Rylo's engineering, product development, and commercial operations teams, alongside continued investment in AI research and workplace accessibility infrastructure.
Andreessen Horowitz led a $30 million Series A financing round for Prosper AI, a healthcare administration platform that, as of June 30, 2025, manages over $1.3 billion in patient care and has achieved fivefold revenue growth. The platform automates scheduling, insurance verification, patient billing, and payer communications, with the company reporting that healthcare providers using the system can reduce administrative costs by more than 40%. Clients include Athenahealth and Jackson Memorial Hospital. Xavier de Gracia, Co-Founder and Co-CEO of Prosper AI, stated that the new capital will fund expansion of its engineering team, deepen integrations with key Electronic Health Record systems, and accelerate market adoption. Jay Rughani, Partner at Andreessen Horowitz, noted the firm was drawn to Prosper AI's ambition to eliminate administrative hurdles across the healthcare continuum, distinguishing it from earlier solutions that focused narrowly on appointment scheduling.
On June 21, 2025, Andreessen Horowitz's crypto investment arm, a16z Crypto, announced a $100 million investment in Digital Asset, the company behind the Canton blockchain. The investment was driven by a conviction that privacy technology will serve as a critical enabler for institutional blockchain adoption. General partner Ali Yahya stated in a blog post that "privacy will be the most important moat in crypto this year," reflecting a broader shift in investor thinking around transaction confidentiality. Canton's native token, CC, holds a market capitalization exceeding $6.3 billion, ranking it as the 15th largest cryptocurrency on CoinMarketCap, trading at approximately $0.16 with a circulating supply of 38.8 billion tokens and no maximum supply cap. The investment follows notable price appreciation in privacy-focused tokens more broadly, with Monero surging over 100% and Zcash rising over 700% at its peak over the prior year.
Andreessen Horowitz also participated in a $25 million investment in Stitch, a Saudi financial services startup, with the announcement made on June 17, 2025. Mohamed Oueida, Founder and CEO of Stitch, described the investment as significant not only for the company but for the broader Middle East technology ecosystem. He stated, "It is a significant investment, and it is a big deal for us, and there's no shying away from that. It does represent tremendous validation for us, and our business model. It's not just a Stitch story, it's a regional one." The funding will be used to accelerate Stitch's geographic expansion across the Gulf Cooperation Council, Egypt, and Southeast Asia, including recent market entries in the Philippines. Oueida identified legacy infrastructure and systems fragmentation as core challenges facing financial institutions, noting that many banks fail to achieve adequate returns on their technology investments. Stitch aims to address these inefficiencies through a unified platform built on a single operating system. The company's partnership with LuLu Exchange in the UAE was cited as an example of its ability to enable successful product launches and cross-market expansion.
Sequoia Capital partner Luciana Lixandru, speaking on June 23, 2025, described Europe as entering a new phase in its technology evolution, one she characterized as "act two" for the region's startup ecosystem. Lixandru, the first European partner at Sequoia Capital, noted that having worked with companies such as Deliveroo, UiPath, and Vinted, she has observed a marked improvement in founder quality across the continent. She pointed to companies like Pennylane and ElevenLabs as examples of experienced entrepreneurs building category-defining businesses in Europe. Lixandru identified artificial intelligence as an equalizer enabling European founders to compete globally, while also acknowledging persistent structural challenges including ecosystem fragmentation and limited cross-border talent mobility. She called for regulatory reforms to support the emergence of generational technology companies capable of reaching trillion-dollar scale.
Sequoia Capital partner Shaun Maguire, on June 16, 2025, articulated the view that SpaceX's public offering — valued at $75 billion at IPO pricing of approximately $135 per share, with post-IPO valuation surpassing $2 trillion — would catalyze a migration of talent from established research institutions and software companies into hardware-focused sectors including biology, energy, and climate technology. Maguire noted that a substantial proportion of hardware startups he currently evaluates already count SpaceX alumni among their founding teams, drawing a parallel to the entrepreneurial wave that followed PayPal's public listing. Sequoia's own stake in SpaceX grew from approximately $2 billion to over $20 billion following the IPO, representing a tenfold return on investments made since 2019. Maguire also highlighted his firm's involvement with other ventures associated with Elon Musk, including xAI, Neuralink, The Boring Company, and X. He cautioned, however, that execution challenges remain significant across these sectors, particularly around regulatory timelines in biology, capital intensity in energy, and policy sensitivity in climate technology.
Sequoia Capital led a $56 million funding round for Astrocade, a startup developing AI-powered tools that allow non-technical users to create playable video games from simple text descriptions, with the round announced on May 5, 2025. The platform uses generative AI to produce game logic, art assets, and mechanics, positioning itself as a potential breakthrough in consumer-facing game creation — a space that previous tools including HyperCard, Flash, and Roblox's Lua-based Studio failed to fully democratize. Sequoia's backing reflects confidence in Astrocade's strategy to integrate both game creation and gameplay within a single platform, potentially circumventing the distribution limitations that constrained earlier content creation tools. The platform faces ongoing challenges around content quality and user retention, as generative AI can rapidly produce functional games but ensuring sustained engagement beyond initial curiosity remains an open question.
Accel India co-led a ₹120 crore (approximately $12.7 million) Series C funding round for Apna Mart, an omnichannel franchise-driven grocery and FMCG chain, alongside existing investor Fundamentum, with additional participation from Peak XV Partners, as announced on June 30, 2025. The round follows Apna Mart's $25 million Series B in March 2025, also led by Fundamentum and Accel. Regulatory filings indicate that Accel India and Fundamentum will each invest ₹55 crore, with Peak XV Partners contributing ₹10 crore, through the issuance of 2,367 Series C compulsorily convertible preference shares at ₹5,06,757 each. Apna Mart's valuation is projected to reach approximately ₹1,470 crore, nearly double the ₹738 crore valuation established in its previous round. Founded by Abhishek Singh and Chetan Garg, the Gurugram-based company operates a franchise-led quick commerce model targeting Tier II and Tier III cities, delivering groceries in approximately 10 minutes through a neighborhood store model. The company reported operating revenue of ₹185 crore and a net loss of ₹76 crore in FY25, and claimed revenue growth of 2.5 times to ₹500 crore in FY26 without disclosing net profit figures.
On June 29, 2025, Accel India led a Rs 130 crore Series A funding round for Oolka, a Bengaluru-based credit management startup, valuing the company at approximately Rs 730 crore. Accel India invested Rs 87.22 crore in the round, with existing investors Lightspeed and Z47, formerly known as Matrix Partners, contributing Rs 20.87 crore and Rs 20.86 crore respectively. Meesho co-founders Vidit Aatrey and Sanjeev Barnwal each participated with investments of Rs 32.65 lakh. Founded in 2024 by Utkrishta Kumar, Oolka is developing an AI-powered credit management platform to help users track, manage, and improve credit health. The company previously raised $7 million in a seed round and had reached nearly 2 million users and processed over Rs 100 crore in credit repayments at the time of that raise, with partnerships in place with Yes Bank, IDFC First Bank, and AU Small Finance Bank. Oolka remains pre-revenue as of FY25, reporting revenue of Rs 5.52 lakh against a loss of Rs 5.58 crore. Competitors in the space include GoodScore, OneScore, Paisabazaar, and CRED.
Also on June 29, 2025, Accel joined Y Combinator and a group of technology executives in backing Pocket, the AI hardware device developed by Open Vision Engineering, Inc., in an $11 million funding round. Other participants included Guillermo Rauch, Mati Staniszewski, and Kaz Nejatian. Pocket is a dedicated device designed to capture conversations and convert them into concise summaries, speaker-attributed notes, and actionable follow-up items, with HIPAA and SOC 2 compliance and end-to-end encryption. The company officially launched in October 2025, shipping over 10,000 devices on its launch day, and had shipped more than 35,000 devices as of the announcement, achieving an annualized revenue run rate of $27 million as of March 2025. Pocket was the standout launch of the Y Combinator Winter 2026 cohort. CEO and co-founder Akshay Narisetti stated, "We started Pocket because we believe the best technology should help people be more present, not more distracted." Cecilia Wang, Partner at Accel, said the firm sees Pocket as defining a new category of AI-native devices, adding, "The team has combined exceptional product vision with impressive execution, building something people love and use every day."
