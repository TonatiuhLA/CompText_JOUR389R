Rylo Raises $85M to Scale AI Communication Platform
Rylo has raised $85 million in new funding. The round was led by General Catalyst's Customer Value Fund. Canaan also led new investment participation. Existing investors Vertex Ventures and Contour joined the round. The funding brings total capital raised above $100 million. The company was previously known as Nagish.
It has now been rebranded as Rylo. The company builds AI communication tools for the Deaf and hard-of-hearing community. It focuses on real-time accessibility solutions. It aims to improve communication in daily life and workplaces. Rylo operates from Tel Aviv and New York.
It plans to expand engineering and product development teams. The company will also scale commercial operations. The funding supports broader product expansion. It also strengthens long-term infrastructure development. Investors see strong demand in accessibility technology.
AI Platform Expands Real-Time Communication Tools
Rylo builds an AI-powered communication platform. It delivers real-time captioning for phone calls. It also supports in-person transcription. The system converts speech into text instantly. It also enables text-to-speech responses.
It improves accessibility in personal and professional settings. The platform includes AI features for communication context. These include sentiment analysis and speaker recognition. It also includes fraud detection tools.
Rylo Sign supports real-time sign language translation. It was developed after the acquisition of Sign.mt in 2025. The system translates between spoken and signed languages. The platform is designed for everyday communication.
It helps users navigate work meetings and public interactions. It reduces dependency on third-party interpretation services. The company builds tools around real user feedback. It focuses on reliability and accuracy in communication.
It aims to create a seamless user experience. The system supports multiple communication channels. It is designed to improve independence for users. It also enhances accessibility in high-pressure environments.
