General Catalyst, the Cambridge, Massachusetts-based global investment and transformation firm founded in 2000, continues to make headlines alongside its venture capital peers, with a series of significant transactions and portfolio developments spanning the final weeks of June 2025. Managing over $30 billion in assets and with a portfolio that includes transformative companies such as Airbnb and Stripe, General Catalyst has been at the center of deals spanning asset management privatization, children's AI infrastructure, and accessibility technology. Meanwhile, peers Andreessen Horowitz, Sequoia Capital, and Accel have each recorded notable investment activity of their own across healthcare AI, blockchain privacy, fintech, gaming, and hardware.
Janus Henderson Group completed its take-private transaction on June 30, with Trian Fund Management, General Catalyst, and Qatar Investment Authority as the principal parties to the deal. Under the terms of the transaction, public shares were converted into cash at $52.00 per share and subsequently delisted from the New York Stock Exchange. The privatization is intended to support long-term investments in technology, investment solutions, and talent development, with the current management team remaining in place under Chief Executive Officer Ali Dibadj. Operational bases in London and Denver will be maintained. Dibadj described the transition as a transformative opportunity, highlighting the potential the firm sees in its new partnerships. Trian's CEO Nelson Peltz noted the significant progress Janus Henderson has made since Trian's initial investment in 2020, while General Catalyst's CEO Hemant Taneja emphasized the firm's commitment to enhancing Janus Henderson's technological capabilities and growth strategy. The transaction was supported by a group of global investors that included MassMutual and Sun Hung Kai & Co.
On June 17, Gurugram-based Omli announced the close of a $1.5 million seed funding round led by India Quotient, with General Catalyst participating. Founded in 2025 by Shivam Munshi and Siddhant Garg, Omli is developing AI infrastructure specifically designed for children's speech and language development, a segment largely underserved by existing commercial speech recognition systems, which are predominantly trained on adult voices. The company's flagship application, OmliKids, allows children aged three to ten to engage in voice-based conversations with an AI companion named Doro and has recorded over 500,000 downloads on the Google Play Store since its launch. To address the technical limitations of conventional models when applied to children's voices, Omli developed VedAnsh-v1, a proprietary speech-to-text and voice activity detection system that has demonstrated materially lower word error rates compared to existing commercial alternatives. Co-founder Siddhant Garg stated the company's goal is to achieve adult-grade accuracy in children's speech recognition, including support for Indian accents and regional languages. With a team of approximately 15 employees, Omli intends to develop next-generation speech models and expand into real-time conversational intelligence systems. India Quotient's Madhukar noted that children represent the largest consumer segment for which AI solutions have yet to be meaningfully developed.
General Catalyst also led an $85 million growth funding round for Rylo, formerly known as Nagish, through its Customer Value Fund. Canaan, Vertex Ventures, and Contour participated in the round, which brings Rylo's total funding beyond $100 million. Headquartered across Tel Aviv and New York, Rylo develops AI-powered communication solutions for Deaf and hard-of-hearing individuals, offering real-time captioning for phone calls, speech-to-text conversion, text-to-speech capabilities, and in-person transcription. Following its acquisition of Sign.mt in 2025, the company launched Rylo Sign, which enables real-time translation between spoken and sign languages. The platform also incorporates speaker recognition, sentiment analysis, and fraud detection capabilities. The new capital will fund expansion across engineering, product development, and commercial operations, in addition to AI research and infrastructure investment.
Among General Catalyst's peers, Andreessen Horowitz reported three investment announcements of its own. Prosper AI closed a $30 million Series A round led by Andreessen Horowitz as of June 30, with revenue having grown fivefold and the platform now managing over $1.3 billion in patient care. Prosper AI's platform covers scheduling, insurance verification, patient billing, and payer communications, with the company reporting that healthcare providers using the system reduce administrative costs by more than 40 percent. Clients include Athenahealth and Jackson Memorial Hospital. Co-Founder and Co-CEO Xavier de Gracia stated that the new capital will be directed toward engineering team expansion and deeper integrations with Electronic Health Record systems. Jay Rughani, Partner at Andreessen Horowitz, noted the firm was drawn to Prosper AI's ambition to eliminate administrative hurdles across the full patient journey.
Andreessen Horowitz's crypto investment arm, a16z Crypto, announced on June 21 a $100 million investment in Digital Asset, the company behind the Canton blockchain. The investment reflects the firm's view that privacy technology will become a primary enabler of institutional blockchain adoption, as financial institutions and asset managers increasingly regard transparent ledgers as a liability. Canton's own token, CC, carries a market capitalization exceeding $6.3 billion, placing it 15th among cryptocurrencies by market cap on CoinMarketCap, with a circulating supply of 38.8 billion tokens. Privacy-focused tokens have experienced significant appreciation, with Monero surging over 100 percent and Zcash gaining over 700 percent at its peak over the past year. Ali Yahya, a general partner at a16z Crypto, stated in a recent blog post that privacy would be the most important competitive advantage in crypto, reflecting the firm's view that protecting transaction details is essential to building broader participation in blockchain ecosystems.
On June 17, Andreessen Horowitz also announced a $25 million investment in Stitch, a Saudi financial services startup. Mohamed Oueida, Founder and CEO of Stitch, described the investment as significant not only for his company but for the broader Middle East region, calling it a validation of Stitch's business model and ambitions. The capital will primarily fund expansion across the Gulf Cooperation Council, Egypt, and Southeast Asia, including recent market launches in the Philippines. Oueida identified fragmented, legacy-burdened infrastructure as the central challenge facing financial institutions today, arguing that despite heavy technology spending, return on investment has not kept pace. Stitch is developing a unified platform to reduce fragmentation and enable financial institutions to unlock the full potential of AI. The company has already partnered with LuLu Exchange in the UAE, which Oueida cited as an example of Stitch's ability to deliver transformation at scale.
Sequoia Capital's activity during the same period included a notable conversation on June 23 with Luciana Lixandru, the first European partner at the firm, who articulated a vision for Europe's emergence as a destination for trillion-dollar technology companies. Having worked with Deliveroo, UiPath, and Vinted, Lixandru observed that European founders have demonstrated the capability to build billion-dollar businesses and are now positioned for even more substantial ambitions. She cited Pennylane and ElevenLabs as examples of founders with established tech backgrounds building innovative solutions in Europe, and identified artificial intelligence as an equalizer enabling European entrepreneurs to compete globally. Lixandru acknowledged ongoing challenges, including fragmented ecosystems and limited cross-border talent mobility, and called for regulatory changes to support the growth of large-scale companies across the region.
Sequoia Capital partner Shaun Maguire, speaking on June 16, argued that SpaceX's public offering at a valuation of $75 billion, with post-IPO valuation surpassing $2 trillion, would accelerate the movement of talent from established research institutions into hardware startup sectors focused on biology, longevity, energy, and climate technology. Maguire noted that a significant proportion of hardware startups already count SpaceX alumni among their founding teams, drawing a parallel to the entrepreneurial wave that followed PayPal's public listing in the early 2000s. Sequoia's own stake in SpaceX grew from approximately $2 billion to over $20 billion following the IPO, representing a tenfold return on the firm's investment of approximately $1.2 billion since 2019. Maguire acknowledged execution risks in each of the sectors he identified, including regulatory timelines in biology, capital intensity in energy, and policy sensitivity in climate technology.
Sequoia Capital also led a $56 million funding round for Astrocade, announced on May 5, backing a startup that enables non-technical users to create playable games using generative AI tools. The investment reflects Sequoia's focus on companies capable of occupying large market categories with distinct distribution advantages. Astrocade generates playable game logic, art assets, and game mechanics from simple natural language descriptions, positioning itself as a potential mass-market alternative to prior game creation tools such as HyperCard, Flash, and Roblox's Lua-based Studio, none of which successfully penetrated the broader consumer market. The company faces core challenges around retention and game quality, and will need to implement curation and iteration systems to sustain engagement beyond initial novelty. Comparisons with Roblox illustrate both the opportunity and the difficulty, as Astrocade currently lacks an existing community ecosystem to bootstrap growth.
Accel reported three investments during the period. On June 30, franchise-driven omnichannel grocery chain Apna Mart announced it is set to raise ₹120 crore (approximately $12.7 million) in a Series C round co-led by Accel India and Fundamentum, with additional participation from Peak XV Partners. Accel India and Fundamentum will each contribute ₹55 crore, with Peak XV contributing ₹10 crore. The round is expected to bring Apna Mart's valuation to approximately ₹1,470 crore, nearly double its prior valuation of ₹738 crore. Founded by Abhishek Singh and Chetan Garg, the Gurugram-based startup targets Tier II and Tier III cities through a franchise-led omnichannel quick commerce model, competing with Blinkit, Swiggy Instamart, and Zepto. The company reported operating revenue of ₹185 crore and a net loss of ₹76 crore in the fiscal year ending March 2025, and claimed revenue growth of 2.5 times to ₹500 crore in FY26 without disclosing net profit.
Accel India led a separate Series A round of Rs 130 crore for Bengaluru-based credit management startup Oolka on June 29, valuing the company at approximately Rs 730 crore. Accel India contributed Rs 87.22 crore, with existing investors Lightspeed and Z47 contributing Rs 20.87 crore and Rs 20.86 crore respectively. Meesho co-founders Vidit Aatrey and Sanjeev Barnwal also participated with individual contributions of Rs 32.65 lakh each. Founded in 2024 by Utkrishta Kumar, Oolka operates an AI-powered credit management platform helping users track and improve their credit health, with prior partnerships established with Yes Bank, IDFC First Bank, and AU Small Finance Bank. The company remains in the pre-revenue stage as of FY25, having reported revenue of Rs 5.52 lakh against a loss of Rs 5.58 crore, with FY26 results not yet submitted. Oolka competes with GoodScore, OneScore, Paisabazaar, and CRED in the credit monitoring and financial guidance segment.
Also on June 29, Accel participated in an $11 million funding round for Open Vision Engineering, the AI hardware company behind Pocket, a dedicated wearable device that captures conversations and converts them into concise summaries, speaker-attributed notes, and actionable follow-up items. Y Combinator and several technology executives also joined the round. Pocket shipped over 10,000 devices on its launch day in October 2025 and had shipped more than 35,000 devices total as of March 2026, achieving an annualized revenue run rate of $27 million at that time. The device became the standout product of the Y Combinator Winter 2026 cohort and has gained traction with prosumer and enterprise clients, including DoorDash. CEO Akshay Narisetti stated that Pocket was built on the belief that the best technology should help people be more present rather than more distracted. Accel Partner Cecilia Wang described Pocket as defining a new category of AI-native devices, citing the team's product vision and execution as key differentiators, and expressed the firm's intent to support the company's scale toward millions of users.
